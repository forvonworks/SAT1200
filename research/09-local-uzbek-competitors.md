# Local Uzbekistan SAT Prep Competitors — Deep Research

**Research date:** 2026-10-25
**Scope:** Uzbekistan-based SAT prep providers — tutoring centers, university-run courses, independent mentors, book/material publishers — found via English- and Uzbek/Russian-language web search.

**Methodology note / limitation:** This research was done entirely through web search snippets. The sandboxed research environment's network policy blocks direct page fetches to `.uz` domains and `t.me` (Telegram) — every attempt to open satashkent.uz, t.me/s/satashkent, kun.uz, afisha.uz, etc. directly returned a policy-level connection refusal (403 at the egress proxy), not a content problem. All findings below therefore come from search-engine-indexed snippets of these pages, not from reading the full pages myself. This means: (a) some figures are fragmentary rather than complete page reads, (b) anything described as "self-reported" below is the company's own marketing copy as indexed, not independently verified, and (c) a human on an unrestricted connection should ideally re-verify pricing/branch details directly on satashkent.uz, the Telegram channels, and Instagram before the business-strategy conclusions are finalized.

## Market context: why local SAT prep demand has exploded

Before the competitor profiles, one macro fact matters more than any single competitor: **the Uzbek government now gives extra state-funded university grant seats to applicants with SAT scores of 1200+** (75% of max). Under Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 578 (13 September 2025), these extra grant seats are set at up to 50% of each bachelor's program's normal admission quota (one infoedu.uz article instead cites a flat 500-seat quota, which conflicts with the official formula — treat the 50%-of-quota figure as primary). Certificates had to be submitted electronically via code "35787 DI" to the national assessment agency by 25 June 2026 for the 2026/2027 intake; a similar mechanism applies to master's admission in the tested subject area.

This is a crucial strategic fact: SAT prep in Uzbekistan is no longer solely about going abroad. It is now also a shortcut into **domestic, state-funded university seats**, which plausibly explains the recent surge of small SAT centers, Telegram "SAT mentors," and news coverage of district-level students posting big scores (Namangan, Fergana, Andijan, Qashqadaryo, Navoi). This also means the addressable market for a Uzbek-market SAT book is broader than "students applying to the US" — it includes a much larger pool of students chasing the local grant.
Sources: [oliygoh.uz grant conditions](https://oliygoh.uz/post/sat-sertifikati-bilan-qoshimcha-davlat-grantiga-qabul-qilish-shartlari-belgilandi), [anhor.uz](https://anhor.uz/news/abiturient-3/), [infoedu.uz](https://infoedu.uz/satdan-1200-ball-olganlarga-davlat-granti-beriladi), [uza.uz](https://uza.uz/ar/posts/sat-sertifikatiga-ega-abituriyentlar-uchun-qoshimcha-davlat-granti-kimlar-imtiyozdan-foydalana-oladi_874799)

## SATashkent

The most established and visible local SAT brand; a direct, scaled competitor.

**What they offer:**
- Offline (in-person) group SAT courses and online group courses, plus 1-on-1 online tutoring.
- A proprietary online practice platform described as "32,000+ real Bluebook-format questions."
- A separate **AP (Advanced Placement) prep arm** (ap.satashkent.uz) offering 16 AP subjects, all online — Calculus AB/BC, Physics 1 & C, Microeconomics, Statistics, Computer Science A, Biology, Chemistry, World History (some "coming soon"). This shows SATashkent is expanding beyond SAT into a broader US-admissions test-prep brand.
- A bundled "SAT + Admissions" package: SAT course plus college-admissions/essay/application counseling.
- Branded math study guides ("qo'llanmalari") — a Fergana-area student's published account of his own SAT prep named "SATashkent math guides" as one of several resources he used (alongside official College Board materials and Erica Meltzer's English-prep books), confirming SATashkent produces and distributes its own in-house written material, not just classes.

**Pricing (self-reported, inconsistent across their own pages — flagged explicitly below):**
- Offline group course: ~1.2M UZS for one subject (SAT prep guide page) vs. 2.0M UZS cited on their Yandex Maps listing for "SAT + Admission" — these are not necessarily the same package, which may explain the gap.
- Online group course: ~1.0M UZS for one subject, ~1.6M UZS for two subjects.
- 1-on-1 online tutoring: $450 for 12 sessions, one subject.
- AP courses: $125/month for one subject; discounted to $100/month for existing SATashkent students/graduates.
- "Free" admissions counseling is advertised with wildly different headline values across their own pages: $5,000 on the SAT-prep-guide page, $1,300 on one part of the homepage, $1,500 on another part of the homepage. This internal inconsistency is itself a notable finding (see Mistakes to Avoid).

**Scale / reach claims (self-reported, unverified):**
- "10,000+ students taught."
- "Students in 32 countries" (homepage claim) — likely referring to destination countries of admitted alumni, not where students live.
- 4 physical branches per their prep-guide page: Tashkent (two locations — "Shaxriston" and "Drujba"/HQ), Andijan, Bukhara.
- However, their own HR/recruiting page advertises hiring across **7 cities including Fergana, Namangan, and Kokand** — contradicting the "4 branches" claim and suggesting either rapid recent expansion or sloppy/outdated marketing copy (or both).
- "100+ students scored 1500+ in 2025" (prep-guide page) vs. "110+ students scored 1500+ in 2025" (Telegram ad for @satashkentresults) — another small but real inconsistency.
- Claimed median/teacher scores also vary by page: one page says "all teachers scored 1500+," another Telegram ad specifies teachers with 1590+ and a median *student* score of 1450.
- Scholarship program: Telegram channel advertised a "Next FULL SCHOLARSHIP for those taking the SAT in June 2025," which included practice tests, 1-on-1 sessions, and group classes, with a May 2025 application deadline (so this specific round is long closed by the research date).

**Business model:**
- Multi-branch bricks-and-mortar model (Tashkent HQ + regional branches) combined with an online arm, so it captures both Tashkent-premium and regional/lower-cost-of-living customers.
- Tiered pricing (offline > online; group < 1-on-1) and a loyalty discount for AP after SAT ("students and graduates" pricing) — a clear upsell/retention funnel from SAT into AP.
- Results-channel marketing: a dedicated Telegram channel (@satashkentresults) purely for broadcasting high scorers, used as social proof, with paid promotion (found via telegramadsarchive.com / telegramads.it listings, confirming they buy Telegram ad placement — a real marketing budget, not purely organic growth).
- Staffing: actively hiring native-English-speaking Math & SAT teachers with salary ranges advertised publicly (6,000,000–31,000,000 UZS/month), which both signals scale and gives competitors/job-seekers visibility into their pay structure.

**Strengths / what they emphasize:**
- Breadth: SAT + AP + admissions counseling under one brand — a "one-stop shop" for the full US-university pipeline, not just test prep.
- A large proprietary question bank (32,000+ Bluebook-format questions) as a specific, concrete claim (vs. vague "our materials are great").
- Named teacher credentials on the AP page (Artem Denisenko, Ayyubkhan, Ollonazar Mukhitdinov, Anvar Ibodullayev) — puts a human face/credibility on instructors.
- Heavy, continuous social proof marketing via the dedicated results channel.

**Weaknesses / gaps found:**
- **Numeric inconsistency across their own pages is the single clearest, most citable weakness** — pricing, branch count, score claims, and admissions-program value differ from page to page of the same website/ecosystem, as detailed above. For a product whose whole pitch is rigor and precision (an exam-prep company), this undermines trust once a prospective customer compares two of their own pages.
- No independent (non-company) reviews, complaint boards, or third-party score-verification were found in this research — all performance claims trace back to SATashkent's own channels.
- Separately and importantly: Uzbek authorities (reported via anhor.uz) broke up an unrelated group in Tashkent that was illegally helping people obtain fraudulent/proxied international SAT certificates by remotely answering test questions. This is **not** alleged to involve SATashkent, but it shows the local SAT-prep market has a real fraud-adjacent reputation risk that any legitimate book/brand should implicitly distance itself from (emphasizing official, legitimate prep).

## "SATashkent Math Book 2" / "SAT Tashkent" math book — inconclusive

The user specifically asked about a topic-based "SAT Tashkent's Math Book 2" reportedly used at a "Stanford School" in Chartak/Namangan. Despite repeated English- and Uzbek-language searches (variations of "SAT Tashkent kitob," "SATashkent matematika," "SAT Toshkent kitob," plus direct searches combining "Stanford School," "Chartak," and "Namangan"), **this specific book could not be located or confirmed as a distinct, independently-indexed publication.**

What was found instead, which likely explains the reference:
- SATashkent is confirmed (see above) to produce and distribute **in-house "matematika qo'llanmalari" (math guides/handbooks)** to its own students — one student's own published account of his prep (from a Fergana-region news story) names these guides as a resource he used, alongside official College Board material and Erica Meltzer's English books. It is plausible that "SAT Tashkent's Math Book 2" is simply the second volume/unit of this unofficial, physically-distributed (not web-indexed) SATashkent handbook series, which a partner or client school (such as a "Stanford" branded private school) obtained and now uses as a classroom text. Several unrelated "Stanford"-branded private schools exist in Uzbekistan (e.g., "Stanford IT School" in Namangan — a private K-12 school, found via Golden Pages — is a plausible candidate for "Stanford School" though its Chartak-district connection and SAT-book usage could not be directly confirmed).
- No separate commercial publisher called "SAT Tashkent" (distinct from "SATashkent" the tutoring center) was found in bookstores (asaxiy.uz, the largest Uzbek online bookstore, had no matching listing), directories, or news coverage.

**Recommendation for the book project:** treat this as weak-but-plausible evidence that SATashkent's internally-distributed, topic/unit-based math guide is circulating informally among schools outside Tashkent (via teacher networks, printed photocopies, or Telegram file-sharing) — which is itself a useful competitive signal: locally-made, topic-based practice booklets are already reaching provincial schools through informal distribution channels that bypass the official bookstore/retail system entirely. A commercially published, properly structured book has a real distribution advantage here if it can tap the same teacher/school networks (see Lessons to Replicate).

Sources: search queries run (no single authoritative source — absence of a result is itself the finding); corroborating context from [talimxabarlari.uz Fergana 1470 report](https://talimxabarlari.uz/72602/) and [Stanford IT School listing](https://www.goldenpages.uz/en/company/?Id=117346)

## New Uzbekistan University (NewUU) — SAT Math / SAT English courses

A university-run (not purely commercial) prep course, notable because it's run by an actual university's Continuing Education Center and includes official SAT test administration.

**What they offer:**
- Separate SAT Math and SAT English courses (newuu.uz/courses/sat-math, /sat-english).
- Covers algebra, geometry, and data analysis plus "problem-solving techniques," with practice tests to track progress.
- Mock exams held at the university's own College-Board-approved SAT Test Center — a genuine structural advantage: students prep and sit mock/real exams at the same institution.
- A placement exam is required before enrollment, and students are grouped by placement result.
- Classes meet 3x/week (Tue/Thu/Sat), with both afternoon and evening shifts.
- A related "Course in Pre-University (CPU)" program also touches SAT and TOEFL ITP prep (page dated Feb 2024, possibly stale).
- Contact via phone, a Telegram bot (@ccecontact_bot), and email (cec@newuu.uz) — a lower-touch, more institutional acquisition channel than the social-media-heavy private centers.

**Pricing:**
- 700,000 UZS/month per subject.
- Discount: 1.2M UZS off if both SAT Math and SAT English are taken together (i.e., a bundling discount).
- Duration: up to 6 months.

**Scale / claims:**
- A 2025 university news item states 40% of NewUU's own applicants had an international SAT certificate — showing how embedded SAT has become in their own admissions pipeline (and why they run the prep course: pipeline-building for their own enrollment, not a pure profit center).
- NewUU has also served as an official SAT exam administration site.

**Strengths:** Institutional credibility, having an actual SAT Test Center on-site, bundling discount, lower price point than most private centers (700K UZS/month vs. SATashkent's ~1–2M UZS course fees).

**Weaknesses / gaps:** Course page for the related Pre-University program appears outdated (Feb 2024); less flexible schedule (fixed 3x/week slots) and a required placement exam creates friction vs. "sign up anytime" private centers; purely institutional/bureaucratic contact channel (no visible social-media community, scores feed, or marketing presence compared to SATashkent's constant Telegram score-announcements).

Sources: [newuu.uz/en/courses/sat-math](https://newuu.uz/en/courses/sat-math), [newuu.uz/ru/courses/sat-math](https://newuu.uz/ru/courses/sat-math), [newuu.uz/en/courses/sat-english](https://newuu.uz/en/courses/sat-english), [newuu.uz/pre-university](https://newuu.uz/pre-university), [newuu.uz/en/news/sat-exam-has-been-conducted-67](https://www.newuu.uz/en/news/sat-exam-has-been-conducted-67), [newuu.uz entrance exams 2025-2026 news](https://www.newuu.uz/ru/news/entrance-exams-for-the-20252026-academic-year-at-new-uzbekistan-university)

## B&S SAT

A smaller, personality-led Tashkent center, founded by two young high-scorers.

**What they offer:** SAT prep (math + English/EBRW implied by "SAT Support Teacher" and "SAT English instructor / SAT EBRW Mentor" job titles); located near Abdulla Qodiriy street, Shaykhantakhur district.

**Founders / credibility marketing:** Founded by **Bobirjon Bakhtiyorov** (self-reported IELTS 9.0, SAT 1600 — a perfect score) and **Sukhrob Murodov** (self-reported IELTS 9.0). Using the founders' own perfect/near-perfect scores as the core trust signal is their primary marketing angle — a "learn from someone who has actually done it" positioning, common among younger/newer local competitors.

**Scale claims (self-reported):** "500+ students have improved their SAT scores," "40+ students reaching 1500+."

**Business model / working conditions (from a job posting):** An intense schedule — "6 days on, 1 off," 15:00–19:00 shifts — suggesting a lean, high-throughput small-business operation rather than a large franchise.

**Weaknesses:** No pricing, course structure, or materials info found at all; no independent reviews found; much smaller apparent footprint than SATashkent (single location, no branch network, no visible proprietary question bank or published materials).

## Independent SAT mentors / personal-brand tutors

A distinct and important category in this market: individual high-scoring young Uzbeks building personal tutoring "brands" (often via Telegram/YouTube) rather than registered companies. These compete directly with centers on price and relatability, especially outside Tashkent.

### Marifat Jamal
- A well-known self-taught SAT tutor; raised her own score from 1420 → 1500 → 1590 through self-study starting in 2019, then built her own SAT academy aimed at helping students — explicitly also government/public-school students — get into US/Canadian universities.
- Notable struggle story used in her own marketing/press profile: her first public masterclass, funded partly from her own borrowed money, drew only ~40 attendees the first session and ~30 the next (far below hoped-for turnout) — a candid, humanizing data point about how hard local market/demand generation actually is, even for a credible high-scorer.
- No enrollment numbers, pricing, or current course structure found.

Source: [kun.uz profile, Nov 2024](https://kun.uz/en/news/2024/11/08/from-student-to-sat-mentor-marifat-jamals-journey-to-sat)

### Parviz Tuksonov — Uzbekistan's first reported perfect SAT score (1600)
- An 18-year-old from Bukhara, reported (Jan 2025) as the first person in Uzbekistan to score a perfect 1600 (first scored 1560 in 2023, retook and hit 1600 in Dec 2024).
- **Crucially, he studied almost entirely with free resources — primarily Khan Academy** — and stated explicitly that there were **no local SAT prep courses or tutors available in his area** at the time he was preparing.
- He went on to informally teach SAT prep to his own classmates after his results.
- This is not a "competitor" in the business sense, but it is a critical market-reality data point: **free, high-quality resources (Khan Academy / College Board Bluebook) are a real, credible substitute for paid local courses**, especially outside Tashkent, and the absence of local tutors in smaller cities is an explicit, stated pain point from a top scorer himself.

Sources: [kun.uz, Jan 2025](https://kun.uz/en/news/2025/01/14/uzbekistans-first-perfect-sat-scorer-shares-his-journey), [fledu.uz](https://fledu.uz/en/a-guy-from-bukhara-scored-the-highest-sat-score-in-the-history-of-uzbekistan), [talimxabarlari.uz](https://talimxabarlari.uz/?p=18007)

### "Learn With US" (Bunyod / @unlocksatmath)
- A personal-brand micro-tutor page (carrd.co one-pager) run by a self-described 8th-grader named Bunyod with a self-reported SAT score of 1270, planning an IELTS exam and SAT retake.
- Telegram channel: t.me/unlocksatmath; personal contact @FlasBerion.
- Illustrates how low the barrier to entry is for "SAT mentor" personal brands in this market — even a middle-schooler with a sub-1300 score can set up a public-facing tutoring page and Telegram channel. This matters for competitive-landscape thinking: the "competition" at the bottom of the market isn't polished businesses, it's a long tail of individual Telegram channels of wildly varying credibility.

Source: [studyieltssat.carrd.co](https://studyieltssat.carrd.co/)

## Institutional / school-based and free/government-backed programs

Not direct commercial competitors, but they shape the market students compare paid options against.

- **WIUT Academic Lyceum SAT Club** (Westminster International University in Tashkent) — ran a school-based SAT Club with a "tailored preparation program" (registration deadline Jan 29, 2024 — likely an annually recurring offering).
- **Millat Umidi Maktabi (Cambridge International College)** — a private high school (grades 10–11) that states it prepares students for SAT and IELTS as part of its regular curriculum, i.e., SAT prep bundled into private-school tuition rather than sold separately.
- **EducationUSA Virtual College Prep Club** (run by EducationUSA Uzbekistan, U.S. Embassy-affiliated) — a multi-month program (historically ~7 months) for grade 9–11 students nationwide, covering TOEFL and SAT test-taking skills plus financing, English writing, and soft skills. A separate "EducationUSA Academy" scholarship (U.S. Embassy-funded) targets 15–17 year-olds. American Corners around Uzbekistan offer free SAT/TOEFL/GRE consultation generally. This is a credible **free, government-adjacent alternative** that a commercial book/course has to implicitly out-compete on quality/convenience, since it can't compete on price.

## Lessons to Replicate

1. **Bundle SAT with the adjacent exam ecosystem.** Nearly every successful local competitor (SATashkent with AP, NewUU with its own admissions pipeline, "Pyat s Plyusom"/Bunker/Real Science with DTM+SAT+IELTS) treats SAT as one module in a bigger "get into a good university" pipeline, not a standalone product. A book series could borrow this logic by explicitly bridging to the national grant pathway (SAT 1200+ → state grant) and not just "get into a US college," since that's now a dual, bigger addressable motivation.
2. **Lead with a concrete, falsifiable proof point.** SATashkent's "32,000+ Bluebook-format questions," B&S SAT's founders' actual 1600/9.0 scores, and the local news cycle's habit of naming individual students' exact scores (1470, 1550, 1590, 1600) all show that *specific, checkable numbers* are what the market responds to and media/word-of-mouth repeats — vague "comprehensive, high-quality" language is not what spreads.
3. **Topic-based, unit-structured practice books are already informally circulating and trusted** (SATashkent's own "matematika qo'llanmalari," reportedly used by at least one student outside Tashkent and apparently reaching schools like a "Stanford"-branded private school). A properly published, polished topic-based Book 2 fills a format gap that currently only exists as informal/photocopied/Telegram-distributed material — there is a real opportunity to be the first *professionally produced* version of something the market has already validated informally.
4. **Regional underserved demand is real and documented by the regional students themselves**, not just inferred: Uzbekistan's first 1600 scorer explicitly said there were no local tutors/courses in his area and that free resources filled the gap. Namangan, Fergana, Andijan, Qashqadaryo, and Navoi all have individual reported high scorers but very little visible commercial infrastructure. A book (cheap, scalable, no need for a physical branch) is structurally the right product for exactly this underserved-regional gap that bricks-and-mortar centers (SATashkent, Bunker, Real Science) have not filled, and it directly rivals the free-resource (Khan Academy) option by being Uzbek-context-aware and physically available where internet/English fluency may be a barrier.
5. **Use founder/mentor credibility as a trust shortcut**, the way B&S SAT and Marifat Jamal both do (explicit perfect/near-perfect scores attached to named real people) — this consistently outperforms generic institutional branding in a market this is this young, social-media-driven, and short on third-party verification infrastructure (no Yelp/Google-reviews culture evident in the research for these businesses).
6. **A dedicated "results/social proof" channel works and is worth the ad spend.** SATashkent runs and pays to promote a dedicated @satashkentresults Telegram channel purely for score announcements. A book launch could use the same playbook: a continuously-updated public channel of real (consented, verifiable) student score improvements tied to the book.

## Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Don't let your own marketing contradict itself.** SATashkent's single biggest, most citable weakness found in this research is that its own pages disagree with each other — on pricing (1.2M vs 2.0M UZS), branch count (4 vs. 7-city hiring), admissions-program value ($5,000 vs $1,300 vs $1,500), and score claims (100+ vs 110+ students at 1500+; "all teachers 1500+" vs "teachers 1590+"). For a company selling rigor and precision, this is a credibility leak that a careful competitor (or a careful book publisher) should specifically avoid: keep every public-facing number (pricing, claims, stats) in one controlled, single source of truth.
2. **Don't rely only on directory/legal registration for visibility — many real, active competitors (SATashkent itself, B&S SAT, Texnik School, independent Telegram mentors) are entirely absent from the official Yellow Pages "SAT preparation" business category**, which lists only 4 generic companies. A book/brand's marketing plan should assume formal directories, government registries, and even app-store-style "official" listings are not where this market actually discovers providers — Telegram, Instagram, OLX classifieds, and word-of-mouth/news coverage of individual top scorers are where the real discovery happens.
3. **Don't ignore the reputational shadow of exam fraud.** A real criminal case (Uzbek tax/law-enforcement breaking up a group illegally helping people fraudulently obtain SAT certificates) shows this market has a trust problem that any new entrant should proactively and explicitly distance itself from — e.g., by being transparent that the book is pure legitimate skill-building with no shortcuts, no "guaranteed score" promises, and clear sourcing of all practice material.
4. **Don't underestimate free resources as "not real competition."** Uzbekistan's own first perfect scorer beat every paid local center using Khan Academy alone, and explicitly said there was no local alternative. A Uzbek SAT book's pitch must therefore justify its value *relative to completely free, legitimate alternatives* (Khan Academy, College Board Bluebook) — not just relative to other paid centers — likely by leaning into localization (Uzbek-context explanations, alignment with the local grant pathway, physical/offline access) that Khan Academy does not offer.
5. **Don't assume Tashkent-centric pricing and positioning works regionally.** Every regional (non-Tashkent) listing found was thin, outdated, or missing pricing/course detail entirely — suggesting that the handful of existing regional "centers" are small, under-resourced, and not aggressively marketed. A book that prices and positions itself the way SATashkent or Bunker School would (premium Tashkent in-person-course pricing) risks being inaccessible or irrelevant to the much larger, underserved regional audience that is currently self-studying with free tools.
6. **Don't publish a pure theory book with no practice bank when the market standard is "thousands of practice questions."** SATashkent's headline differentiator is a specific large question count (32,000+); national news coverage obsesses over exact scores; the market is numerically-minded. A book that can't cite a comparably concrete practice-volume number risks reading as thin next to the local market's quantified-marketing norm.
