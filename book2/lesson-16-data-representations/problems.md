## Set 1 — Reading a Frequency Table

A survey of $50$ students asked each student, "What is your favorite subject?"
The results are shown in the table.

| Subject | Number of students |
|---|---|
| Math | 18 |
| Physics | 9 |
| Biology | 13 |
| History | 10 |
| **Total** | **50** |

1. According to the table, how many of the students surveyed selected physics as their favorite subject?
2. What percentage of the students surveyed selected math as their favorite subject?
3. How many fewer students selected history than selected biology?
4. What is the total number of students who selected either physics or history?
5. If $10$ more students join the survey and all of them select physics, what is the new total number of students surveyed, and what percentage of that new total selected physics?

## Set 2 — Two-Way Table: Row-Based Percentages

$90$ students were surveyed about their gender and preferred sport. The results are shown in the table.

| | Basketball | Volleyball | **Row total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Girls** | 14 | 26 | **40** |
| **Boys** | 33 | 17 | **50** |
| **Column total** | **47** | **43** | **90** |

1. What percentage of the girls surveyed selected volleyball?
2. What percentage of the boys surveyed selected basketball?
3. What is the ratio of the row total for girls to the number of boys who selected volleyball, written in simplest form?
4. What percentage of the boys surveyed selected volleyball?
5. Within the girls' row, the percentage who selected basketball is $35\%$ and the percentage who selected volleyball is $65\%$. What must these two percentages add up to, and why?

## Set 3 — Two-Way Table: Column-Based Percentages

Use the same table as in Set 2.

1. Of the students who selected basketball, what percentage were girls?
2. Of the students who selected volleyball, what percentage were boys?
3. The answer to Set 2, Question 1 ($65\%$, the percentage of girls who selected volleyball) is not the same number as the answer to Question 2 above. Explain why these two percentages don't need to match.
4. What percentage of the total number of students surveyed were boys?
5. When a question asks for "the percentage of boys who selected basketball," which value should be used as the denominator: $33$, $47$, $50$, or $90$? Justify your choice and compute the percentage.

## Set 4 — Mixed: Two-Way Table + Percentage Context

$140$ customers were surveyed about their age group and whether they were satisfied with an online purchase.

| | Satisfied | Not satisfied | **Row total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ages 13–17** | 45 | 15 | **60** |
| **Ages 18–25** | 52 | 28 | **80** |
| **Column total** | **97** | **43** | **140** |

1. What percentage of the customers in the 18–25 age group were not satisfied?
2. Of the customers who were not satisfied, what percentage were in the 13–17 age group?
3. If the number of customers in the 13–17 age group grows by $20\%$ next year, what will the new total for that group be?
4. What is the difference between the number of satisfied customers in the two age groups, and which group's count should be subtracted from which to get a positive result?
5. What percentage of all the customers surveyed were satisfied?

## Set 5 — Scatterplot

The weekly exercise time, in hours ($x$), and the distance run, in kilometers ($y$), for $7$ students are given as the ordered pairs $(2,4),\ (3,5),\ (4,7),\ (5,8),\ (5,10),\ (7,12),\ (8,13)$.

1. As $x$ increases, how does $y$ change? What type of association does this describe?
2. Two of the data points share the same $x$-value of $5$ but have different $y$-values, $8$ and $10$. What does this indicate about real-world data?
3. Based on the trend in the data, approximately how far, in kilometers, would a student who exercises $6$ hours per week be expected to run? (Give a reasonable interval, not a single exact value.)
4. Does the data directly support the conclusion that exercising more causes a person to run farther? Why or why not?
5. Based on this data, why would it be risky to predict the distance run by a student who exercises $25$ hours per week? (Name the concept this illustrates.)

## Set 6 — Spot the Error

Each item below contains exactly one error. Identify the error, name the concept it relates to, and give the correct answer.

1. Using the table from Set 2–3. Question: "Of the students who selected volleyball, what percentage were girls?" *Incorrect solution:* "$\frac{26}{40}=65\%$, so the answer is $65\%$."
2. Same table. Question: "What percentage of the girls selected basketball?" *Incorrect solution:* "$\frac{14}{90}\approx15.6\%$."
3. Based on the data in Set 5: "There is a positive association between exercise hours and distance run, so exercising causes an increase in running ability."
4. Using the table from Set 4. Question: "Of the customers who were not satisfied, what percentage were in the 18–25 age group?" *Incorrect solution:* "$\frac{28}{80}=35\%$."

## Set 7 — Bonus / Challenge ($3\times3$ table)

$120$ students were surveyed about their language-learning method and the level of result they achieved.

| | Low | Medium | High | **Row total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Course method** | 8 | 22 | 10 | **40** |
| **App method** | 12 | 18 | 10 | **40** |
| **Self-study method** | 15 | 15 | 10 | **40** |
| **Column total** | **35** | **55** | **30** | **120** |

1. Of the students who achieved a "High" result, what percentage used the "Self-study" method?
2. What percentage of the students who used the "Course" method achieved a "Low" result?
3. For each method separately, calculate the percentage of students using that method who achieved a "High" result. Compare the three percentages: is the result surprising?
4. What percentage of all $120$ students surveyed achieved a "Medium" result?
5. If $20$ more students join the "App method" group and all of them achieve a "High" result, what is the new total for the App method group, and what is the new total for the "High" column?

## Answer Key

**Set 1:** 1) $9$  2) $36\%$  3) $3$ fewer  4) $19$  5) new total
$=60$, physics $=19/60\approx31.7\%$

**Set 2:** 1) $65\%$  2) $66\%$  3) $40:17$  4) $34\%$  5) $100\%$ —
because the percentages within a single row always sum to the whole row,
which is $100\%$.

**Set 3:** 1) $\approx29.8\%$ ($14/47$)  2) $\approx39.5\%$ ($17/43$)
3. The denominators are different: in Set 2 Q1 the denominator is the girls'
   row total ($40$), while here the denominator is the volleyball column
   total ($43$); the two questions ask about entirely different groups, so
   there's no reason for the percentages to match.
4. $\approx55.6\%$ ($50/90$)
5. The denominator is $50$ (the boys' row total), because the question asks
   for the percentage of BOYS who selected basketball — the percentage is
   taken within the boys' group. $33/50=66\%$.

**Set 4:** 1) $35\%$  2) $\approx34.9\%$ ($15/43$)  3) $72$ ($60\times1.2$)
4) $7$ — $52-45$; subtract the smaller group (ages 13–17, $45$) from the
   larger group (ages 18–25, $52$) to get a positive result.
5) $\approx69.3\%$ ($97/140$)

**Set 5:** 1) Positive association  2) Real data doesn't fall on a perfect
line — different outcomes at the same $x$-value are normal
3) Approximately $10$–$12$ km, based on the trend in the data
4) No — correlation does not prove causation; other factors may also be
   involved
5) Risky, because this is extrapolation — the given data only covers the
   range of $2$–$8$ hours, and $25$ hours falls outside that range

**Set 6:**
1. Error — the denominator used was the girls' row total ($40$), but the
   question asked for the percentage "of the students who selected
   volleyball," so the denominator should have been the volleyball column
   total ($43$). Correct: $26/43\approx60.5\%$.
2. Error — the denominator used was the overall total ($90$), but the
   question asked for the percentage "of the girls," so the denominator
   should have been the girls' row total ($40$). Correct: $14/40=35\%$.
3. Error — correlation was treated as causation; other contributing factors
   were not ruled out. Correct conclusion: "there is a positive association
   between exercise hours and distance run," without claiming causation.
4. Error — the denominator used was the row total ($80$), but the question
   asked for the percentage "of the customers who were not satisfied," so
   the denominator should have been the "Not satisfied" column total
   ($43$). Correct: $28/43\approx65.1\%$.

**Set 7:** 1) $\approx33.3\%$ ($10/30$)  2) $20\%$ ($8/40$)
3. Course: $10/40=25\%$, App: $10/40=25\%$, Self-study: $10/40=25\%$ — all
   three are **equal**; even though the raw counts in the "High" column are
   all $10$, this happens here because the row totals ($40$, $40$, $40$)
   are also equal — in another table this would not necessarily happen by
   coincidence, so always calculate rather than relying on appearance.
4) $\approx45.8\%$ ($55/120$)
5) New total for App method $=60$; new total for the "High" column $=50$
   ($30+20$).
