# Book 2 — Lesson 19: Lines, Angles & Triangles

**Source content file**, paired with `problems.md`. Opens Book 2's final domain,
Geometry & Trigonometry (~15% of the real test per `research/02-topic-difficulty-taxonomy.md`).
Geometry hasn't appeared anywhere else in Book 2 so far — this lesson starts the
angle/triangle rule set from scratch, but at Book 2's rigor level, not Book 1's.

**Research grounding:** `research/02-topic-difficulty-taxonomy.md` names similar-triangle
proportionality as the recurring "medium" twist in this domain. `research/04-common-student-mistakes.md`
documents the exact conceptual gap this lesson is built around: students treating similar
triangles as if their sides were *equal* rather than *proportional*, and — even once they
know the sides are proportional — pairing up the *wrong* corresponding sides when writing
the ratio (Virginia DOE finding). Two supplementary web searches (Nov 2025) confirmed this
is the dominant error family for similarity setup, and that the parallel-line/transversal
angle rules have their own matching error family: assuming lines are parallel without
being told so, and confusing alternate-interior angles (equal) with co-interior angles
(supplementary).

## Concept 1 — Angle Relationships (Burchaklar Orasidagi Munosabatlar)

### Definition

*Yig'indisi $90°$ ga teng bo'lgan ikki burchak **to'ldiruvchi burchaklar** deb ataladi.
Yig'indisi $180°$ ga teng bo'lgan ikki burchak **yondosh burchaklar** deb ataladi. Ikki
to'g'ri chiziq kesishganda hosil bo'lgan, umumiy uchga ega bo'lib, bir-biriga qarama-qarshi
joylashgan burchaklar **vertikal burchaklar** deb ataladi va ular doim o'zaro teng bo'ladi.*

<small>(Two angles whose sum is $90°$ are called complementary angles. Two angles whose
sum is $180°$ are called supplementary angles. When two lines cross, the angles directly
across from each other — sharing a vertex but pointing opposite directions — are called
vertical angles, and they are always equal.)</small>

**Notation callout:** an angle is written $\angle ABC$ (vertex in the middle letter) or, when
unambiguous, just the vertex letter alone, $\angle B$. The small square symbol $\llcorner$
in a figure marks a right angle ($90°$) — treat it as given information, not decoration.

### Triangle Angle Sum

*Har qanday uchburchakning uchta ichki burchagi yig'indisi doimo $180°$ ga teng bo'ladi.*

<small>(The three interior angles of any triangle always sum to $180°$.)</small>

$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180°$$

### Parallel Lines Cut by a Transversal

Ikkita parallel chiziqni kesib o'tuvchi uchinchi chiziq **kesuvchi** (transversal) deb
ataladi. Bu kesishishdan hosil bo'lgan sakkiz burchak orasida uch muhim munosabat bor:

| Munosabat (Relationship) | Ta'rif | Qiymat |
|---|---|---|
| Mos burchaklar (Corresponding angles) | Har ikki kesishish nuqtasida bir xil joylashuvdagi burchaklar | **Teng** ($=$) |
| Almashinuvchi ichki burchaklar (Alternate interior angles) | Kesuvchining ikki tomonida, ikki parallel chiziq orasida joylashgan burchaklar | **Teng** ($=$) |
| Bir tomonlama ichki burchaklar (Co-interior / same-side interior angles) | Kesuvchining bir tomonida, ikki parallel chiziq orasida joylashgan burchaklar | **Yig'indisi $180°$** |

**Shart muhim:** bu uchta munosabat **faqat chiziqlar parallel bo'lgandagina** ishlaydi.
Agar masala chiziqlarning parallel ekanini aytmasa yoki belgilamasa (odatda o'q belgisi
$\rightarrow$ bilan ko'rsatiladi), bu munosabatlarni qo'llash mumkin emas.

### Model Example (1-misol)

Ikki parallel chiziq — $\ell_1$ (yuqoridagi) va $\ell_2$ (pastdagi), $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$
— uchinchi chiziq $t$ bilan kesilgan. $t$ chiziq $\ell_1$ ni $P$ nuqtada, $\ell_2$ ni $Q$
nuqtada kesib o'tadi. $P$ nuqtadagi, **ikki chiziq orasida va kesuvchining o'ng
tomonidagi** burchak (P ning pastki-o'ng burchagi) $115°$ ga teng. $Q$ nuqtadagi, **ikki
chiziq orasida va kesuvchining chap tomonidagi** burchakni (Q ning yuqori-chap burchagini)
toping.

**Yechish:** Ikkala burchak ham ikki parallel chiziq orasida (ichki burchaklar) joylashgan,
ammo kesuvchining **qarama-qarshi tomonlarida** ($P$ da o'ng, $Q$ da chap). Bu aynan
almashinuvchi ichki burchaklarning ta'rifi, demak ular teng. Qo'shimcha hisob-kitob kerak
emas.

**Javob:** $115°$

*(Agar savol o'rniga $Q$ ning yuqori-**o'ng** burchagini so'ragan bo'lsa, javob boshqacha
bo'lardi: bu burchak $P$ dagi $115°$ bilan **bir xil tomonda** (ikkisi ham o'ng) joylashgan
ichki burchaklar juftini hosil qiladi — bir tomonlama ichki burchaklar, ular yig'indisi
$180°$, demak $180°-115°=65°$. Xuddi shu rasmda, savol qaysi burchakni so'raganiga qarab,
javob $115°$ yoki $65°$ bo'ladi — shuning uchun "qaysi burchak haqida so'ralmoqda?" rasmni
diqqat bilan o'qishni talab qiladi.)*

### Your-Turn Example (O'zingiz bajaring)

Xuddi shu rasmda ($\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$, kesuvchi $t$), $P$ ning pastki-o'ng burchagi
$115°$. $Q$ ning **pastki-o'ng** burchagini toping (bu $P$ dagi $115°$ bilan, ikkisi ham
bir xil — pastki-o'ng — holatda joylashgani uchun, mos burchaklar juftini hosil qiladi):
$\underline{\hspace{25mm}}$

### Common Mistake Box (conditional — documented error, include)

**Noto'g'ri yondashuv:** chiziqlar parallel ekanligini rasmda "to'g'ri ko'rinishi"ga qarab
taxmin qilish, yoki almashinuvchi ichki burchaklarni bir tomonlama ichki burchaklar bilan
aralashtirib, ularni teng deb emas, $180°$ ga yig'indisi deb hisoblash (yoki aksincha).

**To'g'ri qoida:** parallel chiziqlar faqat masala aniq aytganda yoki rasmda $\rightarrow$
belgisi bilan ko'rsatilganda qo'llaniladi — hech qachon rasmning "ko'rinishi"ga ishonmang.
Almashinuvchi ichki burchaklar har doim **teng**; bir tomonlama ichki burchaklar har doim
**$180°$ ga yig'indi beradi**. Ikkisini aralashtirmaslik uchun: almashinuvchilar kesuvchining
**qarama-qarshi** tomonlarida, bir tomonlamalar **bir xil** tomonida joylashgan.

## Concept 2 — Triangle Similarity & Correct Correspondence (O'xshash Uchburchaklar)

### Definition

*Mos burchaklari o'zaro teng va mos tomonlari o'zaro proportsional bo'lgan ikki uchburchak
**o'xshash uchburchaklar** deb ataladi.*

<small>(Two triangles whose corresponding angles are equal and whose corresponding sides
are proportional are called similar triangles.)</small>

$$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \angle A=\angle D,\ \angle B=\angle E,\ \angle C=\angle F,\quad \frac{AB}{DE}=\frac{BC}{EF}=\frac{AC}{DF}$$

**This is the lesson's core payoff, so slow down here:** "o'xshash" (similar) does **not**
mean the sides are *equal* — it means they are *proportional*, scaled by one constant
factor. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$ with sides $3,4,5$ and $6,8,10$ are similar
(scale factor $2$), not congruent; their sides are never equal to each other, only their
**ratios** are equal.

### Three ways to prove similarity (no new measuring needed)

| Criterion | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **AA** (Angle-Angle) | Two pairs of corresponding angles are equal (the third pair is automatic, since angles sum to $180°$) |
| **SAS** (Side-Angle-Side) | Two pairs of corresponding sides are proportional **and** the angle between them is equal |
| **SSS** (Side-Side-Side) | All three pairs of corresponding sides are proportional |

### The correspondence trap — reading the ORDER of letters correctly

When a problem writes $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$, the **order of the letters tells
you exactly which vertex matches which**: $A\leftrightarrow D$, $B\leftrightarrow E$,
$C\leftrightarrow F$. This order is not decoration — it is the entire instruction for which
sides to pair in the ratio. The single most common error with similar triangles is correctly
knowing the sides are proportional, then pairing the **wrong** sides anyway, because the
letter order was ignored.

**Rule of thumb:** side $AB$ (between the 1st and 2nd listed vertices) corresponds to side
$DE$ (between the 1st and 2nd listed vertices of the other triangle) — match by *position
in the name*, not by which side "looks like" it matches in a drawing, since SAT figures are
not guaranteed to be drawn to scale.

### Model Example (2-misol)

$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF$. $AB=6$, $BC=9$, $AC=12$. $\triangle DEF$ da $DE=8$.
$EF$ va $DF$ ni toping.

**Yechish:** Harflar tartibiga ko'ra mos tomonlar: $AB\leftrightarrow DE$,
$BC\leftrightarrow EF$, $AC\leftrightarrow DF$. Avval masshtab koeffitsientini topamiz:
$\dfrac{DE}{AB}=\dfrac{8}{6}=\dfrac{4}{3}$.

Bu koeffitsientni qolgan tomonlarga qo'llaymiz. $EF$ — $BC=9$ ga mos keladi:
$EF = 9\times\dfrac{4}{3}=12$. $DF$ — $AC=12$ ga mos keladi: $DF=12\times\dfrac{4}{3}=16$.

**Javob:** $EF=12$, $DF=16$

*(E'tibor bering: $EF$ ni $AB=6$ ga yoki $DF$ ni $BC=9$ ga bog'lab hisoblash — bu aynan
harflar tartibini e'tiborsiz qoldirish xatosi bo'lardi, chunki rasmda uchburchaklar
burilgan yoki aks ettirilgan holda chizilgan bo'lishi mumkin.)*

$\triangle PQR \sim \triangle XYZ$. $PQ=5$, $QR=7$, $PR=10$. $XYZ$ da $XY=15$. $YZ$ va $XZ$
ni toping: $YZ=\underline{\hspace{15mm}}$, $XZ=\underline{\hspace{15mm}}$

### Common Mistake Box (conditional — documented error, include)

**Noto'g'ri yondashuv:** o'xshash uchburchaklarning tomonlari **teng** deb o'ylash (masalan,
$\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF$ bo'lsa, $AB=DE$ deb hisoblash), yoki tomonlarning
proportsional ekanini bilib, ammo rasmga qarab "qaysi tomon qaysi tomonga ko'proq o'xshaydi"
asosida noto'g'ri juftlikni tanlash — harflar tartibini emas.

**To'g'ri qoida:** "O'xshash" — "teng" emas, "proportsional" degani. Mos tomonlarni har doim
uchburchaklarning **nomidagi harflar tartibi** orqali aniqlang ($\triangle ABC\sim\triangle DEF$
da $A\leftrightarrow D$, $B\leftrightarrow E$, $C\leftrightarrow F$), rasmning qanday
chizilganiga qaramay — SAT rasmlari har doim masshtabda chizilmagan bo'lishi mumkin.

## Notes for the problems file (Sets 1–7)

1. Basic angle relationships (complementary/supplementary/vertical) — pure drill.
2. Triangle angle sum — solve for a missing angle, including algebraic expressions for
   angles (e.g. $2x, x+10, 3x-20$).
3. Parallel-line transversal angles — identify corresponding/alternate/co-interior pairs
   and compute, including at least one item where lines are NOT stated as parallel (testing
   whether the student notices the rule doesn't apply).
4. Similar triangles — correct correspondence drill, letter-order-based, several triangles
   presented in different rotations/orientations to defeat "looks like" pattern matching.
5. Mixed — angle relationships + similarity combined (e.g. find an angle, then use AA to
   justify similarity, then solve a proportion).
6. Spot the error — each item has exactly one broken step, mixing the angle-type confusion
   and the correspondence-mismatch trap from the Common Mistake boxes above.
7. Bonus/Challenge — multi-step problems combining several concepts, timed.
