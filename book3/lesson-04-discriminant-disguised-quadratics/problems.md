## Answer Key

Each entry: answer, a short worked solution, and the trick used.

### Set 1 — Bridge: the core moves

1. **A (Exactly one)** — Move everything to one side: $4x^2-12x+9=0$. Then $D=(-12)^2-4(4)(9)=144-144=0$, so there is exactly one real solution ($x=\frac32$). (Zero comes from forgetting to move the $12x$ across; two comes from reading $(2x-3)^2=0$ as two different roots.)  *Trick: Discriminant: count the solutions*

2. **C ($14$)** — Exactly one real solution means $D=b^2-4(1)(49)=0$, so $b^2=196$ and $b=14$ (positive). (7 solves $b^2=49$ and forgets the $4$; $-14$ ignores $b>0$; 196 is $b^2$, not $b$.)  *Trick: D = 0 means exactly one solution*

3. **B ($1$)** — Set the $y$-values equal: $x^2-4x+4=0$, i.e. $(x-2)^2=0$. The only solution is $x=2$, so the line is tangent to the parabola: exactly one common point. (Two comes from counting the repeated factor twice.)  *Trick: Line and parabola: set equal, read the discriminant*

4. **Ans. 8** — Put $y=x-2$ into $xy=15$: $x^2-2x-15=0$, so $x=5$ or $x=-3$. Since $x>0$, $x=5$ and $y=3$, so $x+y=8$. (The pair $x=-3,y=-5$ would give $-8$.)  *Trick: Substitute into the product*

5. **C ($4$)** — Squaring: $3x+4=x^2$, so $x^2-3x-4=0$ and $x=4$ or $x=-1$. Check: $\sqrt{16}=4$ works, but $\sqrt1=1\ne-1$, so $-1$ is extraneous. Only $x=4$ is a solution. (3 is the sum of both roots; $-1$ is the extraneous root; 5 is their difference.)  *Trick: Radical equation: square, then check*

6. **B ($1$)** — Multiply by $(x-2)(x+2)$: $3(x+2)-12=x^2-4$, so $x^2-3x+2=0$ and $x=1$ or $x=2$. But $x=2$ makes a denominator $0$, so only $x=1$ is a solution. (Two is the trap: it counts the excluded value.)  *Trick: Rational equation: reject excluded values*

7. **C ($2$)** — Let $u=x^2$: $u^2-5u-36=0$, so $u=9$ or $u=-4$. Since $x^2$ cannot be negative, only $x^2=9$ works: $x=3$ or $x=-3$, two solutions. (Four counts the impossible $x^2=-4$; one forgets $-3$.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = x squared*

8. **B ($-2$)** — $(x^2-6x+9)+(y^2+10y+25)=-18+9+25$, so $(x-3)^2+(y+5)^2=16$. The center is $(3,-5)$ and $h+k=-2$. (Reading $(-3,5)$ gives 2; adding absolute values gives 8; $-8$ uses $(-3,-5)$.)  *Trick: Completing the square: read the center*

9. **Ans. 4** — Synthetic division by $3$ on the coefficients $1,-4,2,9$ gives $1,-1,-1$ and remainder $6$. So the quotient is $x^2-x-1$ and $r=6$: $a+b+r=-1-1+6=4$.  *Trick: Polynomial division (synthetic)*

10. **D ($x^{7/6}$)** — $\sqrt[3]{x^2}=x^{2/3}$ and $\sqrt x=x^{1/2}$. Multiplying the same base adds the exponents: $\frac23+\frac12=\frac76$. ($x^{1/3}$ multiplies the exponents; $x^{3/5}$ adds tops and bottoms; $x^{1/6}$ subtracts.)  *Trick: Fractional exponents: add when multiplying*

11. **Ans. 38** — With width $w$: $w(w+5)=84$, so $w^2+5w-84=0$ and $(w+12)(w-7)=0$. The width is $7$ (not $-12$) and the length is $12$, so the perimeter is $2(7+12)=38$.  *Trick: Word problem: area gives a quadratic*

12. **A ($-5$)** — Write it as $3x^2-6x-k=0$: $D=36+12k$. No real solutions needs $D<0$, so $k<-3$. Only $-5$ works. ($k=-3$ gives $D=0$, which is exactly one solution, so the boundary is not allowed; $-1$ and $2$ give two.)  *Trick: Discriminant: strict inequality*

### Set 2 — The discriminant backward: tangents, intersections and parameters

1. **D ($6$)** — Set equal: $x^2-2x+(7-c)=0$. Tangent means $D=4-4(7-c)=0$, so $7-c=1$ and $c=6$. ($-2$ adds the slopes instead of subtracting; 7 is the parabola's $y$-intercept; 10 is the $y$-coordinate of the tangent point $(1,10)$.)  *Trick: Tangent: D = 0 after setting equal*

2. **Ans. 17** — Substitute $y=x+k$: $2x^2+2kx+k^2-36=0$. $D=4k^2-8(k^2-36)=288-4k^2>0$ gives $k^2<72$, so $|k|<8.49$. The integers $-8,\dots,8$ number $17$. (The endpoints are not even integers here, but $\pm9$ fail and the tangent cases are not integers.)  *Trick: Circle and line: substitute, D > 0*

3. **A ($14$)** — The parabola opens downward with its vertex at $x=3$: $y=-18+36-5=13$. The line misses the parabola only when $k>13$, so $14$ works. ($k=13$ touches the vertex; $12$ and $-5$ cut the parabola twice. $-5$ is the $y$-intercept.)  *Trick: Horizontal line versus the vertex*

4. **Ans. 3** — $D=4k^2-4(3k+4)=4(k^2-3k-4)=4(k-4)(k+1)$. $D=0$ gives $k=4$ or $k=-1$, and both give exactly one solution. The sum is $3$. (Stopping at the positive value gives 4.)  *Trick: D = 0 can give two values of the parameter*

5. **C ($10$)** — Set equal: $x^2+6x+(c-2)=0$ and need $D=36-4(c-2)>0$, so $c<11$. The greatest integer is $10$. ($c=11$ gives $D=0$, a tangent. $9$ and $8$ forget to subtract the $2$ from $c$.)  *Trick: Two intersections: D > 0 (strict)*

6. **B ($4$)** — For $k=4$: $2x^2+6x+3=0$ has $D=36-24=12>0$, two solutions. At $k=2$ the $x^2$ term vanishes and $6x+3=0$ has one solution (not a quadratic at all). At $k=5$, $D=36-12(3)=0$; at $k=7$, $D=36-60<0$.  *Trick: A parameter in the leading coefficient*

7. **D ($k>6$)** — Set equal: $x^2-2x+(7-k)=0$, $D=4-4(7-k)=4k-24$. Two points need $D>0$, so $k>6$. ($k=6$ is tangent, so $\ge$ is wrong; $k<6$ reverses the sign; $k>7$ uses the parabola's $y$-intercept.)  *Trick: Two intersections: D > 0*

8. **A ($7$)** — Product $12>0$ means the roots have the same sign; their sum $-k$ must be negative, so $k>0$. Two distinct roots need $D=k^2-48>0$, so $k\ge7$. The least is $7$. ($k=6$ has $D<0$; $-7$ gives two positive roots; $4$ is $3+4$ from a factor pair.)  *Trick: Sign of roots: sum, product and D*

9. **D ($9$)** — Substitute $y=-x+k$: $2x^2-2(k+1)x+(k-1)^2+4-18=0$ has $D=0$ exactly when $(k-3)^2=36$, so $k=9$ or $k=-3$. Since $k>0$, $k=9$. ($-3$ is the other tangent line; 3 is the center's coordinate sum; 6 is $|k-3|$ before finishing.)  *Trick: Tangent to a circle: D = 0*

10. **A ($-9$)** — Expand and move $k$: $x^2-4x-5-k=0$. $D=16+4(5+k)=0$ gives $k=-9$. (It is the minimum value of $(x+1)(x-5)$, at the vertex $x=2$. $-5$ is the constant term, 2 is the vertex's $x$-coordinate, 9 is a sign slip.)  *Trick: Disguised quadratic: expand first*

11. **B ($1$)** — Set equal: $x^2-(6+k)x+(9+3k)=0$ has $D=(6+k)^2-4(9+3k)=k^2$. $D=0$ only for $k=0$. The point $(3,1)$ is the vertex of the parabola, so the only tangent through it is the horizontal line; every other slope cuts the parabola twice. (Two is the trap, from thinking of a point with two tangents.)  *Trick: Line through the vertex*

12. **C ($12$)** — An $x$-intercept needs a real solution of $x^2-7x+k=0$: $D=49-4k\ge0$, so $k\le12.25$. The greatest integer is $12$. (13 rounds up; 12.25 is the bound but is not an integer; 7 is $-b$.)  *Trick: At least one: D ≥ 0*

13. **Ans. 20** — Substitute: $5x^2+40x+(100-r^2)=0$. $D=1600-20(100-r^2)=0$ gives $20r^2=400$, so $r^2=20$. (Check by distance: $\frac{10}{\sqrt5}=\sqrt{20}$.)  *Trick: Tangent: the discriminant contains the radius*

14. **Ans. 8** — Two distinct real roots need $D=36-4c>0$, so $c<9$. Both roots have the same sign when the product $c>0$, and the sum $6>0$ makes them positive. So $c=1,2,\dots,8$: eight values. ($c=9$ gives one solution; $c=0$ gives the root $0$, which is not positive.)  *Trick: Sign of roots: D > 0 and product > 0*

### Set 3 — Equations that become quadratics: radicals, fractions, quadratic form, word problems

1. **D ($9$)** — Isolate: $\sqrt{x+7}=x-5$. Squaring: $x+7=x^2-10x+25$, so $x^2-11x+18=0$ and $x=2$ or $x=9$. Check: $x=9$ gives $4+5=9$; $x=2$ gives $3+5=8\ne2$, so it is extraneous. The sum is $9$. (11 adds both roots; 2 is the extraneous root; 18 is the product.)  *Trick: Isolate the radical, square, check*

2. **Ans. -1** — Square: $x^2-2x-k=0$, $D=4+4k$. For $-1<k<0$ it has two positive roots, both valid (two solutions); for $k<-1$ none. At $k=-1$: $(x-1)^2=0$, $x=1$, and $\sqrt1=1$ checks. So $k=-1$.  *Trick: Radical with a parameter: D = 0 and check*

3. **A ($-8$)** — Since $x^2-3x-4=(x-4)(x+1)$, multiply by it: $x(x+1)+3(x-4)=20$, so $x^2+4x-32=0$ and $x=4$ or $x=-8$. But $x=4$ makes a denominator $0$, so the only solution is $-8$. ($-4$ adds both roots; $4$ is the excluded root.)  *Trick: Rational equation: LCD and excluded values*

4. **Ans. -18** — Multiply by $(x-3)(x+3)$: $(x+3)+k=2(x-3)$, so $x=k+9$. This is a solution unless it is excluded: $k+9=3$ gives $k=-6$, and $k+9=-3$ gives $k=-12$. The sum is $-18$.  *Trick: Rational with a parameter: the solution lands on an excluded value*

5. **B ($19$)** — Let $u=x^{1/3}$: $u^2-u-6=0$, so $u=3$ or $u=-2$. Cube: $x=27$ or $x=-8$. The sum is $19$. (1 is the sum of the $u$-values; 27 forgets the negative root; 35 adds $27+8$.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = cube root of x*

6. **C ($3$)** — $9^x=(3^x)^2$. With $u=3^x$: $u^2-12u+27=0$, so $u=3$ or $u=9$, giving $x=1$ or $x=2$. The sum is $3$. (12 is the sum of the $u$-values; 9 is one $u$; 2 is the product of the $x$-values.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = 3 to the x*

7. **B ($\dfrac54$)** — Let $u=\frac1x$: $u^2-5u+4=0$, so $u=1$ or $u=4$. Then $x=1$ or $x=\frac14$, and the sum is $\frac54$. (5 is the sum of the $u$-values; $\frac14$ is the product of the $x$-values; 4 is one $u$.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = 1/x*

8. **D ($2$)** — $-5t^2+30t+5=45$ gives $t^2-6t+8=0$, so $t=2$ or $t=4$. Above 45 m between those times: $4-2=2$ seconds. (3 is the time of the peak; 4 is the later crossing alone; 6 is when the ball is back at the launch height.)  *Trick: Projectile: two crossing times, subtract*

9. **C ($18$)** — Call them $n-1,n,n+1$: $3n^2+2=110$, so $n^2=36$ and $n=6$ (positive). The integers are $5,6,7$ ($25+36+49=110$) and their sum is $18$. (6 is only the middle integer; 36 is $n^2$.)  *Trick: Consecutive integers: middle one as n*

10. **A ($6$)** — If A takes $t$ hours, B takes $t+3$: $\frac1t+\frac1{t+3}=\frac12$, so $2(2t+3)=t(t+3)$, $t^2-t-6=0$ and $t=3$. Then B takes $3+3=6$ hours. (3 is pump A; 5 is $2+3$; 9 adds 3 to the wrong value.)  *Trick: Work rates add: a fraction equation becomes a quadratic*

11. **D ($5$)** — $(20+2x)(30+2x)=1200$ gives $4x^2+100x-600=0$, so $x^2+25x-150=0$ and $(x+30)(x-5)=0$. The positive root is $5$. (10 solves $(20+x)(30+x)=1200$, one border only; 25 is the middle coefficient; 30 is the negative root with its sign dropped.)  *Trick: Border on all sides: both dimensions grow by 2x*

12. **Ans. 4** — Put $x=2y+4$ into $xy=48$: $2y^2+4y-48=0$, so $y^2+2y-24=0$ and $y=4$ or $y=-6$. Then $x=12$ or $x=-8$. The sum of the $x$-coordinates is $12+(-8)=4$.  *Trick: Substitute into the product*

13. **A ($2$)** — $9^x=3^{2x}$, so $2x=x^2-3$, i.e. $x^2-2x-3=0$ and $x=3$ or $x=-1$. The sum is $2$. (Forgetting $9=3^2$ gives $x=x^2-3$, with sum 1; 3 is one solution; $-3$ is their product.)  *Trick: Same base first, then a quadratic*

14. **Ans. 6** — If the first speed is $v$, then $\frac{24}{v}-\frac{24}{v+2}=1$. Multiply by $v(v+2)$: $48=v^2+2v$, so $v^2+2v-48=0$ and $(v+8)(v-6)=0$. So $v=6$ (times $4$ h and $3$ h).  *Trick: Distance = rate x time: a quadratic in the speed*

### Set 4 — Equivalent expressions and completing the square

1. **B ($x^2+4$)** — With $u=x^2$: $u^2-13u+36=(u-4)(u-9)$, so $x^4-13x^2+36=(x^2-4)(x^2-9)=(x-2)(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)$. So $x+2$, $x-3$ and $x^2-9$ are factors, but $x^2+4$ is not (it would come from the sign error $(x^2+4)(x^2+9)$).  *Trick: Factor in quadratic form, then split fully*

2. **C ($2x-3$)** — Group: $x^2(2x-3)-4(2x-3)=(2x-3)(x^2-4)=(2x-3)(x-2)(x+2)$. So $2x-3$ is a factor. (The roots are $\frac32,\,2,\,-2$, so $2x+3$ is a sign slip, $3x-2$ swaps the numbers, and $x+3$ uses a root that does not exist.)  *Trick: Factor by grouping*

3. **Ans. 11** — $6x^2\div2x=3x$: subtract $3x(2x-3)=6x^2-9x$, leaving $10x-12$. $10x\div2x=5$: subtract $5(2x-3)=10x-15$, leaving $3$. So $a=3$, $b=5$, $r=3$ and the sum is $11$. (Using the root $3$ instead of $\frac32$ gives the wrong remainder $33$.)  *Trick: Polynomial long division with a non-monic divisor*

4. **D ($\dfrac{x+21}{x^2-9}$)** — Use $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$: numerator $=2(x+3)-(x-3)+12=2x+6-x+3+12=x+21$. (Writing $-(x-3)$ as $-x-3$ gives $x+15$; dropping the $12$ gives $x+9$; adding denominators gives $2x$.)  *Trick: Common denominator: distribute the minus sign*

5. **A ($x^2+x+1$)** — $x^4+x^2+1=(x^4+2x^2+1)-x^2=(x^2+1)^2-x^2=(x^2+1-x)(x^2+1+x)$. So $x^2+x+1$ is a factor. (Each other choice leaves a nonzero remainder.)  *Trick: Rewrite for a hidden difference of squares*

6. **B ($3$)** — The product is $(x^2+4)^2-a^2x^2=x^4+(8-a^2)x^2+16$. Matching the $x^2$-coefficient: $8-a^2=-1$, so $a^2=9$ and $a=3$. (Dropping the $8$ gives 1; $8-1=7$ and $a^2=9$ are traps.)  *Trick: Spot (A + B)(A - B)*

7. **Ans. 3/2** — $x^4\sqrt x=x^{4+1/2}=x^{9/2}$. The cube root is the power $\frac13$: $\left(x^{9/2}\right)^{1/3}=x^{3/2}$. So $k=\frac32=1.5$.  *Trick: Fractional exponents: inside first, then the root*

8. **C ($3-x$)** — $x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2$, and $\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=|x-3|$. For $x<3$, $x-3$ is negative, so $|x-3|=3-x$. (Choice B is the answer only when $x\ge3$; $|x|-3$ and $x+3$ are not even equal to $|x-3|$ in general.)  *Trick: Square root of a square is an absolute value*

9. **B ($8$)** — Complete both squares: $(x+4)^2+(y-1)^2=k+16+1=k+17$. The radius is 5, so $k+17=25$ and $k=8$. (5 is the radius; 25 is $r^2$; $42$ comes from $k-17=25$.)  *Trick: Completing the square: the right side becomes r squared*

10. **Ans. 30** — Divide by 2: $x^2-6x=-\frac32$. Add $9$: $(x-3)^2=\frac{15}2$. Then $x=3\pm\sqrt{\frac{15}{2}}=3\pm\frac{\sqrt{30}}{2}$, so $b=30$. (Skipping the division by 2 gives the wrong $b$.)  *Trick: Completing the square with a leading coefficient*

11. **D ($1$)** — $(x+3)^2+(y-2)^2=-13+9+4=0$. A sum of squares is $0$ only when both are $0$: $x=-3$, $y=2$. Exactly one point. (This is a circle with radius $0$; it is not a full circle with infinitely many points.)  *Trick: Completing the square: a circle of radius 0*

12. **A ($x^2+3x+9$)** — $x^3-27=(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$, so the quotient is $x^2+3x+9$. Check by multiplying: $(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)=x^3+3x^2+9x-3x^2-9x-27$. (Wrong signs or a dropped middle term are the usual traps.)  *Trick: Difference of cubes: a³ − b³*

13. **Ans. 6** — Multiply by $(x-2)(x+1)$: $5x-1=A(x+1)+B(x-2)$. At $x=2$: $9=3A$, so $A=3$. At $x=-1$: $-6=-3B$, so $B=2$. Then $AB=6$.  *Trick: Equate numerators; plug in the excluded values*

14. **C ($8$)** — Four equal terms: $4\cdot4^x=4^{x+1}$. Write the right side with base 4: $2^{18}=4^9$. So $x+1=9$ and $x=8$. (9 forgets the $+1$; 4.5 divides $18$ by $4$; 16 subtracts $2$ without halving.)  *Trick: Same base: combine like terms first*

### Set 5 — Summit: the hardest mixed problems

1. **Ans. 14** — Squaring: $x^2-9x+(16-k)=0$ with $D=17+4k$. A solution must also satisfy $x\ge4$. For $k\le-5$: no real roots. For $k=-4$: roots $5$ and $4$, both valid (two solutions). For $k\ge-3$: the larger root $\frac{9+\sqrt D}{2}>4$ is valid, but the smaller root $\frac{9-\sqrt D}{2}<4$ is extraneous, so exactly one solution. The integers $-3,\dots,10$ number $14$.  *Trick: Radical + discriminant: which roots survive the check?*

2. **B ($5$)** — Substitute $y=2x+b$: $5x^2+4bx+(b^2+2b-15)=0$. $D=16b^2-20(b^2+2b-15)=-4(b^2+10b-75)=-4(b+15)(b-5)=0$, so $b=5$ or $-15$. Since $b>0$, $b=5$. ($-15$ is the other tangent; $15$ is the constant on the right; $10$ is a middle step.)  *Trick: Tangent to a circle: substitute, D = 0*

3. **C ($3$)** — $2^{3-x}=\dfrac{8}{2^x}$. With $u=2^x$: $u+\frac8u=9$, so $u^2-9u+8=0$ and $u=1$ or $u=8$. Then $x=0$ or $x=3$, and the sum is $3$. (9 is the sum of the $u$-values; 8 is one $u$; 0 is the product of the solutions.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u and 8/u*

4. **Ans. 9** — With $u=x^2$: $u^2-10u+(9-k)=0$. Each positive $u$ gives two $x$-values, $u=0$ gives one, negative $u$ gives none. Three solutions needs the roots $u=0$ and one positive $u$: $9-k=0$, so $k=9$ (then $u=0$ or $10$, and $x=0,\pm\sqrt{10}$).  *Trick: Quadratic form: one u must be zero*

5. **D ($5$)** — Let $u=x^2-5x$: $u^2+4u-21=(u+7)(u-3)=0$. For $u=3$: $x^2-5x-3=0$ has $D=37>0$ and sum $5$. For $u=-7$: $x^2-5x+7=0$ has $D=-3<0$, no real solution. The sum is $5$. (10 counts both $u$-values as if both gave real roots; $-4$ is the sum of the $u$-values; 3 is one $u$.)  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = x² − 5x, then check each u*

6. **B ($7$)** — $D=k^2-4(k+3)=k^2-4k-12=(k-6)(k+2)<0$ gives $-2<k<6$. The integers $-1,0,\dots,5$ number $7$. (Including $k=6$ or $k=-2$, where $D=0$ gives one solution, produces 8 or 9.)  *Trick: No real solutions: D < 0, a quadratic inequality in k*

7. **A ($k=1$)** — For $x\neq1$, $\dfrac{x(x-1)}{x-1}=x$, so the equation says $x=k$. That works for every $k$ except $k=1$, because $x=1$ is not allowed. So $k=1$ gives no solution. (The wrong idea is to cross-multiply and find $x=k$ always works.)  *Trick: Cancel first: the hole in the graph*

8. **D ($48$)** — Let the integers be $n$ and $n+2$: $\frac{2n+2}{n(n+2)}=\frac7{24}$, so $48n+48=7n^2+14n$ and $7n^2-34n-48=0$. This factors as $(7n+8)(n-6)=0$, so $n=6$. The integers are $6$ and $8$, with product $48$. (6 is the smaller integer; 14 is their sum; 24 is the denominator.)  *Trick: Reciprocals of consecutive numbers: LCD gives a quadratic*

9. **C ($16$)** — On the $x$-axis $y=0$, so $x^2-8x+c=0$ must have exactly one solution: $64-4c=0$, $c=16$. (Check: $(x-4)^2+(y+1)^2=17-16=1$, radius $1$ equals the distance from the center $(4,-1)$ to the axis. $c=1$ would make the circle tangent to the $y$-axis; 17 is $r^2+c$.)  *Trick: Tangent to an axis: set y = 0, D = 0*

10. **Ans. 25** — Let $u=\sqrt x\ge0$: $u^2-3u-10=(u-5)(u+2)=0$. $u=5$ gives $x=25$; $u=-2$ is impossible because a square root is never negative. (Squaring $-2$ would give the false root $x=4$, which fails: $4-6-10\ne0$.) The only solution is $25$.  *Trick: Quadratic form: u = √x must be non-negative*

11. **B ($\dfrac34$)** — Substitute: $(1+k^2)x^2-10x+16=0$, $D=100-64(1+k^2)\ge0$, so $k^2\le\frac9{16}$ and $k\le\frac34$. (Geometry check: the radius $3$ and the distance $5$ make a 3-4-5 triangle, so the tangent slope is $\frac34$, not the sine $\frac35$ or the reciprocal $\frac43$.)  *Trick: Line through the origin and a circle: D ≥ 0*

12. **A ($3$)** — For $k\neq0$: $D=16-4k^2=0$ gives $k=2$ or $k=-2$ (one repeated solution each). For $k=0$ the equation is $4x=0$, which also has exactly one solution. So there are $3$ values. (Two forgets the linear case.)  *Trick: Do not forget the case where the equation is not quadratic*

13. **C ($3$)** — Since $x^2=y+4$, the circle becomes $y+4+y^2=16$, so $y^2+y-12=0$ and $y=3$ or $y=-4$. For $y=3$: $x^2=7$, two points. For $y=-4$: $x^2=0$, one point $(0,-4)$. Total: $3$. (Four assumes each $y$ gives two points.)  *Trick: Substitute x² (not y): solve for y first*

14. **D ($2$)** — $2^{3x}\cdot2^{2x+2}=2^{4x+4}$, so $5x+2=4x+4$ and $x=2$. (Forgetting the $2$ in $4^{x+1}=2^{2x+2}$ gives $x=4$; writing $4^{x+1}=2^{2x+1}$ gives $3$; dropping the $+4$ gives $-2$.)  *Trick: Rewrite every power with base 2*
